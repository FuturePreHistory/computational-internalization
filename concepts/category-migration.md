# Category Migration

范畴迁移

## Definition

Category migration refers to a change in the functional position of a computational relation: a relation that initially exists as a model-side judgment criterion, operational object, or external signal comes to acquire efficacy in organizing pre-reflective experience.

The relevant change is therefore not simply that a subject learns to use a technical signal more quickly or automatically. The stronger claim is that a selected computational relation begins to participate in how objects and situations appear similar, salient, approachable, avoidable, expected, or action-relevant before explicit propositional judgment.

A minimal schema is:

`R → I(R) → T → learning and adaptation → E`

where:

- `R` denotes a computational relation maintained by a machine system;
- `I(R)` denotes the target relational structure selected for experiential translation;
- `T` denotes the technical translation mechanism;
- `E` denotes the resulting pre-reflective experiential structure.

Category migration occurs when relevant structure from `I(R)` acquires a stable role in `E`.

## What migrates

What migrates is not necessarily a complete model, representation, concept, or numerical output.

The object of migration may instead be a selected relational structure, such as:

- order;
- neighborhood;
- direction;
- similarity;
- boundary;
- relative magnitude;
- transition structure;
- action-relevant gradient.

Technical translation will normally involve selection, compression, dimensional reduction, or transformation. Category migration therefore does not require strict structural identity between the computational relation and experience.

The relevant question is whether the relational properties selected as significant continue to be systematically preserved and expressed at the experiential level.

## Three joint criteria

Category migration should not be inferred from behavioral automaticity, technical performance, or first-person immediacy alone.

Three kinds of evidence are jointly required.

### 1. Preservation of the target relational structure

The experiential differences produced through `T` should systematically track the properties of `I(R)` that the translation is intended to preserve.

If the target relation contains an ordering, direction, neighborhood structure, similarity relation, or boundary, the experiential organization should preserve the relevant structure within an explicitly defined tolerance.

### 2. Cross-instance generalization

The acquired distinction should extend beyond memorized training pairs.

When the subject encounters new objects, states, or situations, similarity judgments, expectations, bodily preparation, or action tendencies should continue to track `I(R)`.

This criterion helps distinguish relation learning from fixed signal–response conditioning.

### 3. Object–situation manifestation efficacy

The relation should participate in how the relevant object or situation appears before explicit judgment.

Evidence may include systematic changes in:

- perceived similarity;
- salience;
- bodily expectation;
- approachability;
- avoidance tendency;
- action readiness;
- perceived transition or boundary structure.

Interventions on `T`, remapping experiments, removal effects, or conflicts between explicit belief and experiential appearance can provide additional evidence.

No single criterion is sufficient by itself.

## Category migration and technical mediation

Information augmentation, sensory augmentation, neurofeedback, and other technical systems describe ways in which technology intervenes in perception and action.

Category migration describes a different dimension: the functional position ultimately acquired by the translated relation.

The same technical device may remain an external information channel in one use context and, after sustained learning, contribute to pre-reflective experiential organization in another.

Category migration is therefore not a separate hardware category.

It is a claim about experiential function.

## Strong Lambda

The notation `Λ` refers to a stronger limiting case.

A strong `Λ` is a computational relation that:

- is maintained primarily on the machine side;
- cannot be independently reconstructed by the subject;
- lacks a mature conceptual representation that reliably captures its relevant structure;
- cannot be recovered merely by increasing the subject's exposure to the original world or stimulus structure;
- and has practical significance that cannot be stably replaced by a simpler independently accessible proxy.

A strong `Λ` is important because it tests whether technical translation can establish an experiential pathway to a relation that does not already possess an independent perceptual route.

Existing sensory augmentation and decoded-neurofeedback research provide partial precedents for components of this mechanism, but they do not yet establish a fully demonstrated strong-`Λ` case under the criteria used here.

Whether such relations can acquire stable object–situation manifestation efficacy remains an open empirical question.

## The translatable window

Not every computational relation can or should migrate into experience.

For category migration to be possible, the selected structure `I(R)` must satisfy two competing constraints.

It must preserve enough of the original computational relation to retain its practical or epistemic value.

It must also fall within a range that human subjects can learn, generalize, and integrate into experience.

The region in which both conditions can be satisfied is called the translatable window.

For some computational relations, this window may be narrow or empty.

## Epistemic limitation

Successful category migration does not establish that the underlying computational relation is true, reliable, or epistemically justified.

A spurious correlation, biased model, distribution artifact, or defective objective function could also produce a stable and learnable experiential structure.

It is therefore necessary to distinguish:

`migration success`

from:

`epistemic validity`.

The former concerns whether a computational relation has acquired experiential efficacy.

The latter concerns whether the relation deserves to guide belief, action, or institutional judgment.

## Conceptual boundary

“Category” is used here as a working term for the experiential organizational position acquired by a relation.

It is not intended to mean:

- a Kantian category;
- a Husserlian category in any strict technical sense;
- a linguistic concept;
- a machine-learning class label.

Category migration also does not require the subject to possess an explicit concept for the relation.

Experience may change before stable naming, explanation, or theoretical representation becomes available.

## Relation to computational internalization

Category migration describes the transition:

`computational relation → experiential organizational efficacy`.

Computational internalization describes a further structural condition:

the experiential efficacy has become relatively stable while the computational and translational conditions that sustain it remain externally maintained, versioned, and modifiable.

Category migration can therefore occur without full computational internalization.

Computational internalization presupposes some sufficiently stable form of category migration or equivalent experiential efficacy.

## Canonical formulation

Category migration occurs when a selected computational relation moves from functioning primarily as an object of explicit judgment, technical operation, or external signaling to functioning as a stable organizer of pre-reflective similarity, salience, expectation, or possibilities for action.
